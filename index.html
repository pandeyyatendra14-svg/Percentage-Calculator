4. Understanding the Core Percentage Formula

Every calculation on this page boils down to one simple idea: a percentage is a ratio expressed as a fraction of 100. The fundamental formula is (Part ÷ Whole) × 100. Once you understand this, all other percentage problems — increase, decrease, discounts, and averages — become straightforward variations of the same idea.

Frequently Asked Questions & Percentage Formulas

To calculate your percentage (such as for exam marks or test scores), divide the marks obtained by the total possible marks, and then multiply the result by 100.

Percentage (%) = (Marks Obtained ÷ Total Marks) × 100
To find 20% of 70, convert 20% into a decimal (20 ÷ 100 = 0.20) and multiply it by 70:

0.20 × 70 = 14
Divide the part value by the total value and multiply by 100 to get what percentage the part is of the total.

% of Total = (Part ÷ Total) × 100
The core percentage formula calculates the relationship between a part value and a whole value:

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
To find percentage increase, subtract the initial value from the final value, divide by the initial value, and multiply by 100.

% Increase = [(Final - Initial) ÷ Initial] × 100
To calculate the average of a set of numbers, sum up all the numbers and divide by the total count of numbers.

Average = (Sum of Numbers) ÷ (Count of Numbers)
